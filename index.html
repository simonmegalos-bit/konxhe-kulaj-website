About

Biography

Konxhe Kulaj is a postdoctoral researcher at the Department of Pathology, Cancer Center Amsterdam (Amsterdam UMC / Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam), where she investigates cancer extracellular vesicles and immune modulation.

She completed her PhD in Experimental Medicine at the Technische Universität München and Helmholtz Zentrum München in January 2024, under the supervision of Prof. Dr. Kerstin Stemmer at the Institute for Diabetes and Obesity. Her doctoral research centred on adipocyte-derived extracellular vesicles (AdEVs) as inter-organ communicators in obesity and metabolic disease — culminating in a co-first-author publication in Nature Communications demonstrating that AdEVs from obese adipose tissue augment pancreatic β-cell insulin secretion by transferring insulinotropic protein cargo.

Prior to her doctorate, she held a Master's degree in Biology from the University of Konstanz and completed a five-year Biology Diploma at the University of Patras, Greece. An early research internship at the Department of Neuroscience, University of Verona, first introduced her to experimental laboratory science.

Her work spans proteomics, cell biology, mouse models of metabolic disease, and, increasingly, tumour immunology. She is a member of the International Society for Extracellular Vesicles (ISEV) and contributed as a consortium member to the field-defining MISEV2023 guidelines.

Research

Research Themes

Two interlocking research programmes, connected by a common interest in extracellular vesicles as mediators of intercellular communication.

01

Adipose–Pancreas Communication in Metabolic Disease

During her doctoral work, Dr. Kulaj investigated how adipocytes communicate with distant tissues through extracellular vesicles. Her central finding — published in Nature Communications (2023) — demonstrated that adipocyte-derived EVs from obese mice selectively transfer insulinotropic proteins to pancreatic β-cells, augmenting GPCR/cAMP/PKA signalling and amplifying glucose-stimulated insulin secretion. This work established AdEVs as both functional mediators and potential biomarkers of metabolic dysregulation in obesity-linked type 2 diabetes.

02

Cancer Extracellular Vesicles & Immune Modulation

At Cancer Center Amsterdam, her postdoctoral programme focuses on how EVs shape the tumour microenvironment and regulate immune responses. Current projects include examining monocyte–myeloma cell interactions: a 2026 HemaSphere study she co-authored showed that monocytes transfer mitochondria to multiple myeloma cells via CD38–CD31 interactions, enhancing oxidative phosphorylation, cell survival, and drug resistance. The anti-CD38 antibody daratumumab was found to block this transfer, with implications for therapeutic strategy.

03

EV Biogenesis, Characterisation & Standards

As a consortium member of ISEV's MISEV2023 initiative, Dr. Kulaj contributed to the international effort to establish minimal information standards for extracellular vesicle research — a consensus framework now cited over 3,700 times and used by laboratories worldwide. Her ongoing commitment to methodological rigour in EV biology reflects an interest in reproducibility and field-wide standardisation.

Cross-disciplinary Potential & Collaborations

The arc of Dr. Kulaj's research — from metabolic disease to oncology — reflects the growing recognition that EV biology is a unifying lens across pathologies. Potential collaborations span obesity–cancer crosstalk, EV-based biomarker discovery, and therapeutic targeting of EV-mediated intercellular communication. She is open to interdisciplinary exchanges at this intersection.

Adipocyte-derived extracellular vesicles increase insulin secretion through transport of insulinotropic protein cargo

PhD Thesis

Adipocyte-derived Extracellular Vesicles in Metabolic Disease: Regulators of Insulin Secretion with Diagnostic Potential

Technische Universität München / Helmholtz Zentrum München · Awarded 26 January 2024 · Supervisor: Prof. Dr. Kerstin Stemmer

This doctoral thesis explores how adipocyte-derived extracellular vesicles (AdEVs) participate in inter-organ communication between adipose tissue and the endocrine pancreas in the context of obesity and metabolic disease. By combining fluorescence-based vesicle tracing, SILAC proteomics, and phosphoproteomic profiling, the work characterises the protein cargo of AdEVs from lean and obese mice and demonstrates that obese-derived AdEVs selectively augment insulin secretion from pancreatic β-cells — a capacity linked to their distinct insulinotropic protein content. The thesis further examines the diagnostic potential of AdEVs as circulating biomarkers of metabolic dysregulation, contributing a framework for their use in early-stage disease detection.
